In one sentence

HOPE: Survival is a top-down co-op survival game for 1–8 players where the infected follow the noise you make — and when night falls, the horde comes for your base.

Short description

The world is broken. Humanity is fading. But hope still remains. Scavenge a quarantined island alone or with up to seven friends and turn your base into the last warm light in a dying world. Every gunshot draws the infected in. Some nights, the horde comes for the base. Hold until sunrise — every morning is a small victory.

Long description

HOPE: Survival is a top-down survival game set on a quarantined island, played solo or in online co-op for up to eight players.

Noise drives everything. Gunshots, explosions and the machines running at your base draw the infected in, so every fight trades firepower for attention. A thrown stone pulls a crowd away; one careless burst brings the whole street down on the squad.

Days are for scavenging and building: crafting stations, a furnace, extractors, garden beds, gates and traps. Some nights, the horde comes — each one hits harder than the last and grows with the group. Survival goes past a health bar: hunger, thirst, fatigue, temperature, bleeding, broken bones, and food that spoils.

Fight up close with melee combos or at range with firearms, a bow, grenades and molotovs. Fall in the field and you rise as one of the infected, still carrying your gear. Notes scattered across the island tell what happened here.

Key features

  • Solo or online co-op for 1–8 players.
  • Noise as a core mechanic: gunshots, explosions and machines attract the infected; stones lure them away.
  • Night hordes: they don't come every night, but each one hits harder than the last and grows with the squad.
  • Base building: crafting stations, furnace, extractors, garden beds, gates and traps.
  • Deep survival: hunger, thirst, fatigue, temperature, bleeding, broken bones, food spoilage.
  • Melee combos, firearms, bow, grenades and molotovs.
  • Fall in the field and rise as one of the infected — carrying your gear.
  • Environmental storytelling through notes; snow, fog, rain and storms.
